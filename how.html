A trade is a coin with rules

There is no exchange account here and nobody holds your money. The market is four contracts of its own, a shared price oracle, and eleven kinds of transaction. A contract is an address: what is written in it decides the only transactions that can ever spend a coin sent there. Arkade checks those rules before it signs anything.

The four contracts

Two are personal deposit addresses, made from your own terms. Two are shared: every offer lives at one address and every position at another, which is how anyone can list the whole market. Each function below is one way a coin at that address can be spent; follow it to see the transaction.

Every transaction

Coins on the left are spent, coins on the right are created. Each spent coin names the contract function that allows it. A function that nobody has to sign can be submitted by anyone: your page, the other side, a stranger.

The payoff

A position holds both margins. Its resolve and close functions compute how that pot is split from a signed price and the time. Move both to see exactly what they would pay.

  • profit or loss = size × (price − entry) ÷ price, reversed for a short
  • holding fee = size × daily rate × days held, never past the end date
  • provider's share = provider's margin − trader's profit + holding fee, kept between 0 and the whole pot
  • trader's share = the rest; a share of 330 sats or less is folded into the other

Where the terms are written

A deposit's terms are part of its address. Offers and positions share their address, so their terms travel in a short note attached to the transaction that created the coin. A contract reads its own note when it is spent, and only lets a new coin be created if the new note is exactly what its rules compute.

Price note (34)

  • Price in USD cents and the time it was signed
  • Written by the price oracle from the price DIA signed, rounded to the cent

Offer note (64)

  • The provider's key and payout address
  • Spread, holding fee, highest leverage, longest trade

Position note (65)

  • Long or short, entry price, size
  • The trader's margin and the provider's margin
  • Start and end dates, the holding fee
  • The trader's key and both payout addresses

Someone can attach a made-up note to a coin they fund themselves. A position only ever pays out its own sats, so that can only misdirect the forger's money, and this page ignores notes the contracts could not have written.

What you are trusting

  • The Arkade operator. It runs the rules and co-signs every move. It cannot change where a coin may go, but if it disappears, offers and positions have no way out on their own. Deposits do: their owner can exit on-chain.
  • The price. Every price is a BTC/USD update signed by DIA, an oracle network. A separate contract, the price oracle, checks DIA's signature: a transaction that needs a price spends one of the oracle's coins along with its own, and the oracle is put straight back. The market's contracts only check that the genuine oracle was there, which they recognise by a token it alone holds. So no page and no server can make a price up. Two keys could: DIA's, and the oracle's admin key, which can move the token to other rules.
  • Someone showing up. Rules say what may happen, not that it does. A trade opens or settles when someone submits the transaction.
  • Time. Coins expire. A coin made by a transaction expires with the earliest of the coins it spent, so the other side's coin can shorten yours. Renewal exists in the contracts but nothing runs it for you yet.
